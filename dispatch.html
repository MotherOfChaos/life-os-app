Iran Deal Emerging: Hormuz to Reopen, 60-Day Ceasefire Extension on the Table

This is the biggest economic news in months. The US and Iran have developed a framework that would extend their April 8 ceasefire by 60 days, de-mine and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, allow Iran to freely sell oil, and open negotiations on Iran's nuclear programme. The deal was described by a senior US official as largely in place as of today.

Trump said Saturday the agreement was "largely negotiated" and would be announced shortly — then walked it back Sunday saying he "won't rush." Iran's parliament spokesman said Tehran won't yield to threats. Pakistani and Qatari mediators have been shuttling between sides. Markets rallied on the news; oil prices fell sharply.

Israel is strongly opposed to any deal that includes a stop to the Lebanon war. It's the key remaining sticking point. A finalised deal could be announced any day — or collapse.

Why it matters: The Strait handles ~20% of global oil/gas. Reopening it ends the worst energy crisis in decades — EU inflation drops, global growth recovers. Brent crude already sliding on optimism.

Ukraine
Russia Uses Nuclear-Capable Oreshnik Missile in Massive Kyiv Attack — Chernobyl Museum Destroyed

Russia carried out one of its heaviest bombardments of Kyiv since the war began on Sunday, firing an Oreshnik hypersonic ballistic missile — capable of carrying a nuclear warhead — near the capital. It was only the third time the weapon has been used in the four-year conflict. The barrage killed at least 4 people and wounded over 80.

Among the damage: the National Chernobyl Museum was effectively destroyed. The National Art Museum was damaged. Dozens of residential buildings and schools were hit. Zelenskyy: "There must be consequences." Russia said it used Oreshnik, Iskander, Kinzhal and Zircon missiles, claiming to target military facilities.

Significant: The Oreshnik is designed to be nearly impossible to intercept. Its use in a third attack signals Russia is normalising the weapon. NATO foreign ministers met in Helsingborg this weekend — but UK, France, Spain, Italy and Canada blocked a NATO plan for allies to allocate 0.25% of GDP as mandatory Ukraine military aid.

🧠Science & Neuroscience
⭐⭐ Major — HMI
First Neural Evidence of How the Brain Combines Ideas — "Compositional Generalization" Mapped

A study published May 23 provides the first direct neural evidence of compositional generalization — the cognitive capacity to recombine familiar elements into genuinely novel ideas. This is one of the core things that distinguishes human thought from current AI: we can combine "red" and "square" even if we've only ever seen "red circle" and "blue square." Researchers have now identified the specific neural signatures of this process.

The finding is foundational for understanding creativity, language, and abstract reasoning — and for designing AI systems that can actually generalise, rather than interpolate within training data.

Why it matters: This is the cognitive gap between humans and current LLMs made visible at the neural level. Pre-training research (Karpathy's new team at Anthropic) is directly relevant here.
⭐ Cognitive Flexibility
Serotonin Drives Cognitive Flexibility by Reducing "Belief Stickiness"

New research (May 22) shows serotonin is a key driver of cognitive flexibility by actively reducing what researchers call "belief stickiness" — the tendency to cling to outdated assumptions even when evidence contradicts them. The mechanism is neurochemical: serotonin in specific prefrontal circuits lowers the brain's resistance to updating its models of the world.

Clinically relevant for depression, OCD, and rigid thinking patterns. Also speaks to why SSRI antidepressants sometimes improve cognitive adaptability alongside mood.

Alzheimer's: IDOL Enzyme Is a New High-Value Drug Target

Indiana University researchers identified an enzyme called IDOL as a powerful new Alzheimer's drug target. Removing it from neurons significantly reduced amyloid plaques, lowered APOE4 (the strongest genetic Alzheimer's risk factor), and improved neuron-to-neuron communication. Unlike current treatments which clear existing plaques, targeting IDOL addresses plaque production AND cognitive resilience simultaneously.

Also: UC San Diego found women may be especially sensitive to common dementia risk factors — personalised prevention strategies for women could be key (study of 17,000 adults).
B12 "Normal" Levels May Not Protect the Aging Brain — UCSF

Researchers at UCSF found that older adults with B12 levels considered "normal" by current guidelines still showed meaningful cognitive protection gaps. The study suggests that the threshold for what counts as adequate B12 for brain health may need to be significantly revised upward — particularly for people over 60.

🚀Space
China · Launched Yesterday
Shenzhou 23: One Astronaut Will Stay in Space for a Year — First HK Astronaut in Orbit

China launched the Shenzhou 23 spacecraft Sunday night with three astronauts: commander Zhu Yangzhu, Zhang Zhiyuan, and Lai Ka-ying — the first astronaut from Hong Kong, who holds a doctorate in computer forensics. One crew member will remain at the Tiangong space station for a full year, among the world's longest single stays, specifically to study "human adaptability and performance limits" in long-duration spaceflight.

The mission marks another step in China's preparation for its first crewed lunar landing by 2030. China's Tiangong station was built independently after the US blocked China's access to the International Space Station on national security grounds.

Context: NASA aims to land on the Moon in 2028. China aims for 2030. The space race has new protagonists.
🦠Global Health
PHEIC Declared
Ebola Bundibugyo Outbreak: 10 Countries at Risk, Spreading to Kampala

The WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on May 17 after Ebola (Bundibugyo strain) was confirmed in eastern DRC's Ituri Province and in Uganda's capital Kampala. As of May 24, the outbreak has reached Ituri, Nord-Kivu and Sud-Kivu in DRC, with 5 confirmed cases now in Kampala. Over 1,000 contacts are being traced in Ituri alone.

The Bundibugyo strain is rare and has NO available vaccine or specific treatment — distinguishing it from previous outbreaks where the Merck Ebola vaccine was effective. Officials have raised the number of countries at risk from 3 to 10. Community members in DRC attacked and burned a health tent treating patients — a sign of significant community resistance to containment efforts. One American healthcare worker has tested positive and been evacuated to Germany.

Risk for EU/Spain: ECDC rates the likelihood of infection for people in the EU/EEA as "very low." Entry restrictions now apply in the US for recent travellers from DRC, Uganda and South Sudan. No cases in Europe.
