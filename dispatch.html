Carlo Petrini, Founder of Slow Food, Dies at 76

Carlo Petrini, who founded the Slow Food movement in 1986 in protest against McDonald's opening at Rome's Spanish Steps, died Thursday at his home in Bra, Piedmont. He had been diagnosed with prostate cancer in recent years.

Petrini built Slow Food from a small group of friends in the Italian countryside into a global movement in 160+ countries, championing small-scale farmers, biodiversity and the right to "good, clean and fair food." Italian President Mattarella called his passing "a huge void not only in the world of food and wine, but in society as a whole." Named European Hero by Time in 2004; The Guardian listed him among 50 people who could save the world in 2008.

"Those who sow utopia, reap reality." — Carlo Petrini

AI Is Rewiring How Professionals Think — EEG Study Shows Measurable Brain Changes

A new neuroscience study from Lardi & Partner Consulting (Zurich, May 22) used wearable EEG devices to monitor brain activity in professionals performing realistic tasks with and without AI assistance. It tracked five parameters: mental effort, attention, creativity, familiarity, and relaxation — in real time, continuously.

Finding: AI is not just changing what people do at work. It is changing how their brains process the work itself. The study raises questions about long-term cognitive adaptation, skill atrophy, and what "thinking" looks like when a portion of it is routinely offloaded.

Why it matters for M: This is the exact intersection of HMI + neuroscience + AI that defines the frontier. The brain adapts to tools — always has. The question is how we design tools that enhance rather than replace cognition.
