Iran · Day 93
Trump Exits Situation Room Without Signing — Then Sends Back Tougher Nuclear Demands
What happened

On Friday, Trump held a Situation Room meeting saying he would make a "final determination" on the Iran deal. He exited two hours later without signing. He then posted on Truth Social listing Iran's obligations: never possess a nuclear weapon; immediately open the Strait of Hormuz. Trump subsequently sent the draft MOU back to negotiators with changes requesting tougher language on Iran's nuclear commitments — specifically seeking specific concessions beyond Iran's current commitment not to pursue nuclear weapons.

Per Axios (citing two sources), the current MOU text commits Iran to never pursuing nuclear weapons but does not specify concessions beyond that — Trump wants concrete commitments on enrichment suspension and disposal of Iran's enriched uranium stockpile before he signs. The US would negotiate on sanctions relief during the 60-day extension.

Also from Friday: Kuwait intercepted missiles fired from Iran, and the US Treasury Department imposed new sanctions on Iran's military oil sales arm — even as the deal was being discussed.

🧠Science & Neuroscience
⭐ HMI · Settled Debate
A Century-Long Piano Debate Settled: A Pianist's Touch DOES Change Tone Colour
What happened

For over a century, pianists and music teachers have argued about whether a performer's physical touch — the way a key is depressed, not just how hard — can actually change the sonic character of a note. Physicists insisted it couldn't, since a piano hammer's force is determined only by key velocity. New research published May 28 says the answer is yes: touch does influence tone colour, measurably and reproducibly.

The mechanism involves the timing and micro-distribution of force across the key surface affecting hammer contact dynamics in ways previously unmodelled. This has implications for performance pedagogy, instrument design, and haptic interface research — if touch affects sound in a system previously thought to be velocity-only, what other "touch-blind" systems are actually touch-sensitive?

Direct HMI relevance: this challenges the assumption that digital piano interfaces, which are explicitly velocity-only, faithfully reproduce the acoustic piano's expressive range. The gap is physical, not just perceptual.

Cognitive Science
Hippocampal Memory Networks Start Dense and Random — Then Prune Themselves
What happened

Researchers found that early neural networks in the hippocampus — the brain's memory centre — begin life as dense, seemingly random webs of connections that then prune themselves into structured, meaningful patterns. This contradicts the "blank slate" model of memory formation and suggests the brain arrives with far more pre-wiring than thought. The finding has implications for understanding learning differences and potentially for how we think about information acquisition in both humans and AI systems.

⭐ ADHD · Major
Brain Scans Reveal 3 Distinct Types of ADHD — Each Needs a Different Treatment
What happened

A JAMA Psychiatry study analysed brain scans from over 1,150 people and found ADHD is not one condition — it is three distinct neurological types, each rooted in a different brain region, with different symptom profiles and different responses to stimulant medication. This is the first study to identify ADHD types from brain structure rather than symptom checklists.

Critically: stimulant medications did NOT respond the same way across all three types. The frustrating trial-and-error that most people with ADHD experience when finding the right medication may reflect a real biological mismatch — not randomness. The research does not yet establish new treatment protocols but points toward precision medicine for ADHD: a diagnosis guided by neurobiology rather than symptom description.

If you've experienced inconsistent responses to ADHD medication — this explains why. The same drug works differently in brains with different structural profiles. This research lays the groundwork for choosing treatment by brain type, not trial and error.
⭐ ADHD · Evidence
Largest ADHD Treatment Review Ever: Mindfulness Is the Only Intervention With Large Long-Term Benefits
What happened

A sweeping review of over 200 meta-analyses on ADHD treatments (Université Paris Nanterre, published in The BMJ) found that stimulant medication works well short-term but mindfulness was the only intervention to show large benefits at extended follow-up. The team also identified significant misinformation circulating about treatment options — leading people to waste time and money on non-evidence-based approaches while waiting in already long mental health queues.

The researchers developed an interactive public database (ebiadhd-database.org) clearly explaining what each intervention does and doesn't do, based on the review's evidence grades. Limitations: cognitive behavioural therapy in children and mindfulness long-term had limited evidence bases — large effects but smaller study pools.

Concerta (methylphenidate) addresses short-term attention. Long-term: the evidence base for mindfulness specifically — not generic relaxation, but structured mindfulness practice — is the strongest available for sustained ADHD management.
Fibromyalgia
TENS Therapy Reduces Fibromyalgia Pain for 6+ Months — First Real-World Trial Published
What happened

The first real-world clinical trial of transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) for fibromyalgia — published March 27 in JAMA Network Open — found that adding TENS to standard physical therapy significantly reduced movement-based pain and fatigue. The effects lasted at least six months after the trial ended. TENS is safe, inexpensive, widely available, and non-pharmaceutical.

The broader 2026 treatment landscape: low-dose naltrexone (LDN) is attracting strong clinical interest for central sensitisation; transcranial magnetic stimulation shows early promise; regenerative approaches (stem cell) are in early trial phases. Mayo Clinic noted in February 2026 that fibromyalgia research is now focused on the central nervous system's role — shifting from "musculoskeletal condition" to "central sensitisation syndrome" — which changes what treatments make sense.

New UK NHS Wales research confirmed what many patients report but clinicians rarely address: hormonal status — particularly the menopausal transition — is systematically ignored in fibromyalgia care pathways, despite strong evidence that hormonal shifts significantly affect pain severity.

⭐ Menopause · Brain
AAN 2026: Perimenopause Rewires the Brain — Memory Circuits Change in 40s and 50s
What happened

Presented at the 2026 American Academy of Neurology annual meeting (Yale University), MRI and PET studies now show measurable changes in brain connectivity in women in their 40s and 50s directly linked to declining estrogen during the menopausal transition. Memory circuits specifically are affected — the same tightly connected circuits that support efficient memory performance in women may also serve as a faster pathway for tau protein spread in those who develop Alzheimer's disease.

Crucially: perimenopause is a highly dynamic state — brain structural connectivity can actually INCREASE during perimenopause, then decrease again in postmenopause. This is not simple decline. Multiple conditions — migraine, epilepsy, multiple sclerosis, and Alzheimer's — are all affected by the menopausal transition in distinct ways. Migraine often worsens significantly during perimenopause, then changes sharply at postmenopause.

A Cambridge/UK Biobank study (Psychological Medicine) confirmed hippocampus, entorhinal cortex and anterior cingulate cortex volumes are all significantly decreased in post-menopausal women — and that HRT's effects on brain volumes are more complex than previously assumed.

The perimenopause-brain connection is one of the most under-researched areas in neuroscience given how many women it affects. The findings matter for understanding fibromyalgia flares, cognitive changes, and long-term neurological risk — particularly for women managing overlapping conditions.
