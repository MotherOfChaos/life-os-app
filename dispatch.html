Developing
Strait of Hormuz: Still Closed, Global Economy Paying the Price

The Strait of Hormuz has been largely blocked since February 28, when the US and Israel attacked Iran and assassinated Supreme Leader Khamenei. Iran retaliated by closing the strait to international shipping. A US-led aerial campaign has been ongoing since March 19, with a naval blockade of Iranian ports from April 13.

A ceasefire was agreed on April 8, but shipping remains at a practical standstill — just 10 commercial ships per day transiting, versus 130 before the crisis. The UN has cut its global growth forecast; the EU cut its 2026 forecast today, projecting 3.1% inflation, a full point higher than expected.

Scale: One of the world's key oil/gas chokepoints. Every $10 sustained price rise = approx. 0.4% drop in GDP growth globally.

Andalucia: PP Wins But Needs Vox — Sánchez's PSOE Records Historic Low

In the May 17 Andalucian regional election, Juanma Moreno's PP took 53 seats — first place, but 2 short of the 55-seat majority needed to govern alone. Moreno is expected to rely on Vox (15 seats) for support. The surprise of the night was Adelante Andalucia surging from 2 to 8 seats.

The biggest story: PSOE recorded its worst-ever result in Andalucia, falling to 28 seats. Prime Minister Sánchez congratulated the left's effort but acknowledged a difficult result. With 61.9% turnout — notably higher than 2022 — the fragmented parliament now requires coalition negotiations.

Economy
EU Slashes 2026 Growth Forecast — Hormuz Energy Shock Driving Inflation to 3.1%

The European Commission today downgraded its 2026 economic growth forecast, citing the ongoing Strait of Hormuz crisis as the primary driver. EU inflation is now forecast to reach 3.1% — a full percentage point above previous projections — fuelled by surging energy costs. Brussels compared the shock to the 2022 Russian energy crisis but noted the EU is better positioned this time, given years of diversification and renewables investment.

The UN also cut its global growth forecast separately, with the Middle East/North Africa region bearing the heaviest revision (−2.8 points to 1.1%).

🧠 Science & Neuroscience
⭐ ADHD Relevant
Your "Ums" Map Your Brain: Speech Pauses Reveal Executive Function

A study published in May found that everyday speech disfluencies — pauses, filler words, moments of word-finding difficulty — are closely tied to executive function, the exact cognitive domain affected by ADHD. AI analysis of natural conversation can now predict memory capacity, planning ability, and attention control with surprising accuracy. The research suggests speech patterns may serve as a non-invasive diagnostic window into prefrontal cortex function.

Why it matters: This is HMI territory — natural language as cognitive biomarker. Potential for early screening tools that require no specialist equipment.
⭐ Major Finding
Mitochondria Link to Memory Loss — And It's Reversible

Researchers showed for the first time that malfunctioning mitochondria — the cell's energy generators — may directly cause cognitive decline in neurodegenerative disease. Crucially, a new tool that temporarily boosts mitochondrial function reversed memory loss in trials. Published May 16; implications for Alzheimer's and related conditions are significant.

Nature · May 21
Exercise Hormone Protects Neurons in MS and Neurodegeneration

A study in Nature Metabolism found that irisin — a hormone released by muscles during exercise — promotes the survival of neurons in inflammation-driven neurodegenerative conditions, including multiple sclerosis models. Another layer of evidence for exercise as a disease-modifying intervention, not merely symptomatic relief.

Anxiety Linked to Brain Choline Deficit — Especially in Prefrontal Cortex

A major brain scan analysis found people with anxiety disorders show measurably lower choline levels, with the strongest effect in the prefrontal cortex — the region governing emotional regulation and executive control. Choline is a nutrient found in eggs, fish, and legumes. The finding opens dietary and supplement pathways alongside existing interventions.

Spatial Memory Reshapes for Decades — Not Stored, Continuously Rebuilt

New research from Nature Human Behaviour tracked memory for real-world navigational episodes over up to 30 years, finding that spatial memory is not stored statically but continuously reshaped — shifting from episode-specific representations toward generalised schematic knowledge over time. Memory is less a recording and more an ongoing reconstruction.
